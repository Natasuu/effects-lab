# Canvas 2D image hover effects missing from the lab

Scope checked against the lab in `index.html` (10 October 2026). Already shipped, and treated as duplicates here: Pixel burst, Jelly, Magnetic, Liquid ripple, Shatter, Glitch, Halftone, Scatter, Sand, Swirl, Lens, Wave, Pixelate, Highlighter, ASCII, 3D tilt, Dissolve, Black hole, Echo, Focus, Filings, Bulge, Pinch, Glow, Pluck, Confetti, Melt, Spotlight, Drop, Dock zoom, VHS, Duotone, plus the GSAP elastic / explode / scramble / 3D flip / wiggle / inertia / swap / orbit / cascade / smear cards.

Constraint used while judging sources: one vanilla Canvas 2D card, bitmap logo or glyph sprites, hover in then return to rest, no Three.js, no Pixi, no new dependencies. Where the famous version is WebGL, the note says what a 2D approximation loses.

Sixteen candidates are ranked below for how well one effect can sit on both a logo card and a letter-by-letter headline. Rank 1 is the best shared fit. Each entry has a visual, a plain-language algorithm, radius and strength, logo versus letters, and why it is not a cousin of the shipped set.

## Which canvas image hovers keep showing up and are still missing here?

### Takeaway

The effects that keep recurring in Canvas 2D tutorials, and that this lab does not already have, are a local chromatic split, a rim-weighted glass remap, ordered dither, a destructive-then-restored smudge, slat blinds, a pencil-sketch mask, rising ink smoke, cross-stitch, brightness pixel-sorting, pointer slit-scan, a Voronoi mosaic, a corner peel, a Verlet cloth sheet, contour stitching, a moving posterize threshold, and metaball goop. Full-frame RGB channel shift, grid source-offset “liquid,” and repelling pixel particles showed up too, and they are cousins of Glitch, Bulge, or Scatter.

### Cited Findings

Ranked catalog. Radius is how far from the pointer the effect lives. Strength is the second knob, matching the lab’s shared strength control.

1. **Chromatic rim lens.** Inside a soft disc, the red, green, and blue samples separate along the line from the lens center, most at the rim, and lock back together on leave. The sourced full-frame version is different and should not be copied: one channel is replaced by the same channel `intensity` pixels to the right, and `phase` picks which channel, over the whole image ([lqt0223 gist](https://gist.github.com/lqt0223/8a258b68ae1c032fa1fb1e26c4965e8d), demo [Tomas Hubelbauer](https://github.com/TomasHubelbauer/canvas-chromatic-aberration)). The missing fidget is the radial version: three remaps with three different strengths, keeping one channel from each, which is how glass writeups describe dispersion ([Arcaweb liquid glass](https://www.arcaweb.ch/en/recreate-liquid-glass-effect-html-canvas), WebGL description in [liquid-glass-canvas](https://github.com/Whynotmetoo/liquid-glass-canvas)). Radius is the disc. Strength is the pixel offset at the rim, about 2–6 px before a small mark turns to mud. Logos stay recognizable because the split is local. Letters stay readable if the offset is smaller than the stroke; serifs double if it is not. Not Glitch: Glitch slips full-width slices sideways. This never moves a whole row, and the fringe is radial.

2. **Rim-refraction glass.** A rounded droplet follows the pointer. The middle of the droplet is almost the true artwork. The rim bends the sample, and a bright edge sells the glass. Arcaweb’s Canvas 2D method stores the artwork once, and for each droplet pixel computes a normalized distance from the edge (`0` at the edge, `1` in the middle), then a factor `f = 1 + k * (1 - norm)^n`, then reads the source at the center plus `(pixel - center) * f`. Their prose says the power is cubic; the code block uses a square. `k` around `-0.3` is their convex example. They also composite a reflection and a Fresnel-style rim, and they only rewrite the droplet rectangle ([Arcaweb](https://www.arcaweb.ch/en/recreate-liquid-glass-effect-html-canvas)). Radius is the droplet size. Strength is `|k|`. A droplet the size of the whole card collapses into the existing Lens; it works when untouched artwork remains around it. Flat logos read well. Letter counters bend and can close, so the droplet should stay under about one em. Not Lens: Lens is a uniform magnify. Not Bulge: Bulge pushes pieces outward. Here the sample bend is strongest at the rim and the center stays put.

3. **Bayer dither lens.** Under the pointer the artwork quantizes through a tiled threshold matrix, so intermediate tones become a stable dot pattern instead of a flat band. A 4×4 matrix is indexed by `x mod 4`, `y mod 4`; luminance is compared with that cell’s threshold and set to black or white ([javaspring.net Bayer / Atkinson / Floyd–Steinberg](https://www.javaspring.net/blog/monochrome-dithering-in-javascript-bayer-atkinson-floyd-steinberg/)). Spencer Szabados writes the multi-level form in p5: with `c` grey levels and an 8×8 map `M`, each channel becomes `255 * floor(T + value * (c - 1) / 255) / (c - 1)` ([Bayer Dithering, 2022](https://spencerszabados.github.io/blog/2022/bayer-dithering/)). A later canvas helper exposes `levels` and a monochrome switch on `ImageData` ([smw.ai](https://smw.ai/blog/bayer-dither-crt-mask-react)). The 2026 “retro dither” lens that follows a cursor is a WebGL shader with Bayer, hatch, or dash patterns, not a 2D port ([Canvas UI Retro Dither](https://canvasui.dev/docs/components/retro-dither)). Radius feathers original versus dithered. Strength is the level count: 2 is harsh, 4–8 keeps brand color. Both cards work. An 8×8 cell bigger than a small logo destroys it; use 4×4 there. Thin strokes whose luminance sits near the threshold drop out. Not Halftone: dots do not swell with darkness, the matrix is fixed. Not Pixelate: blocks are not a grid average. Not Duotone: color is not remapped onto two brand inks.

4. **Smudge paint.** Dragging the pointer smears the actual pixels along the stroke, like a wet finger, and leaving restores the clean bitmap. The Eckart demo is explicit that its Smudge tool is `getImageData` / `putImageData`, and that it changes the picture rather than an overlay ([Simple Paint Demo](https://math.hws.edu/graphicsbook/demos/c2/SimplePaintDemo.html)). The practical brush from the Stack Overflow thread: on move, copy the patch under the brush, stamp that copy in small steps from the last point to the current point at partial alpha, then grab a new patch; a radial `destination-out` gradient feathers the stamp ([HTML5 Canvas smudge brush](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/28197378/html5-canvas-javascript-smudge-brush-tool)). Radius is the brush. Strength is the stamp alpha and the step size. Both cards work if the brush is a fraction of the mark. A brush wider than a letter fills counters. The lab must keep an untouched buffer, because the sourced tools are destructive and this lab has to return to rest. Not Highlighter: nothing is painted behind the art in a flat accent. Not GSAP smear: pieces are not skewed by velocity; pixels are copied along the path.

5. **Slat blinds.** Horizontal strips of the artwork squash toward their own center line as the pointer approaches, like a slat turning edge-on, then open again on leave. The shipped visual is CSS, not canvas: several clipped copies of one image, each with `transform-origin` on the slat axis, rotate on hover ([the new code, Venetian blind transition](https://thenewcode.com/776/3d-venetian-blind-image-transition-in-pure-css); CodePen [Dimitra Vasilopoulou / JyYoEe](https://codepen.io/mimikos/pen/JyYoEe) via [Armory](https://armory.visualsoldiers.com/venetian-blinds-effect/); a rotateX variant at [AI CSS Animations](https://www.aicssanimations.com/directory/Hover-Effects/venetian-blinds)). Canvas 2D can only scale a strip, using the 9-argument `drawImage` to cut each band ([MDN drawImage](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/CanvasRenderingContext2D/drawImage)). Radius decides which slats are affected. Strength is how close `scaleY` gets to 0. Both cards stay legible if a slat never closes fully. Quality lost versus the CSS 3D versions: no slat thickness, no perspective. Not VHS: rows are not torn sideways. Not Wave: there is no traveling sine. Not Pluck: rows are not springs.

6. **Pencil sketch mask.** Inside the radius the bitmap becomes a soft pencil drawing; outside it stays the photo. The sourced pipeline is grayscale, invert, blur the invert, then color-dodge with the grayscale: `base / (1 - blend / 255)`, with a divide-by-zero guard when the blend channel is 255. The blur is what keeps it from going pure white ([JsonKit, image to sketch](https://jsokit.com/blog/posts/image-to-sketch-gaussian-blur-and-color-dodge-blending-with-canvas/)). A harder cousin is Sobel magnitude, `sqrt(gx² + gy²)`, then a threshold ([collidingScopes/scanlines](https://github.com/collidingScopes/scanlines); convolution writeup [BestHub](https://www.besthub.dev/articles/how-to-build-a-versatile-canvas-library-with-edge-detection-video-capture-and-particle-effects-96bc31ce2cf1)). Precompute the sketch once. Radius feathers the mask. Strength is the blur radius of the inverted layer. Letters, being edges, sketch well. A flat two-color logo has almost no edge and goes blank; that is the main logo failure. Not ASCII, not Focus, not Duotone.

7. **Ink smoke.** Soft puffs are born on opaque pixels near the pointer, rise, drift, grow, and fade. The artwork itself does not move. Middlebury’s fall 2024 smoke tutorial (p5, which draws to canvas) is the algorithm: an emitter, a life that fades alpha, an upward force described as inverse gravity, a mouse force `G / distance²` clamped so it cannot explode, then a blurred sprite at low alpha so puffs only read where they stack. Optional noise breaks up the puff and the air ([CS 467 tutorial 06, fall 2024](https://www.cs.middlebury.edu/~candrews/classes/cs467-f24/tutorials/tutorial06-smoke)). A no-dependency canvas version spawns smoke on `mousemove` ([smoke.js via CSS Script, last noted April 2019](https://www.cssscript.com/interactive-smoke-effect-javascript-canvas-smoke-js/)). Effect.Labs documents the same vanilla pattern: `vy` upward, `x` plus a sine of `y`, size growing, alpha tied to life ([Effect.Labs smoke](https://effect-labs.com/en/effects/particles/smoke.html)). For this lab, tint each puff with the logo pixel at birth, and do not import p5. Radius is the spawn disc. Strength is spawn rate and upward force. Both cards work because the bitmap stays put. Thin letters emit few puffs. A large sprite covers the glyph. Not Sand or Melt or Pixel burst: those remove or move the artwork.

8. **Cross-stitch knit.** Opaque pixels are downsampled into a stitch grid and drawn as X marks in a small thread palette, sewing in across the radius and unswewing on leave. The pipeline in the browser tools is: resize to the stitch grid with `drawImage`, read `ImageData`, quantize, map each cell to a thread color. PhotoToPattern describes width-in-stitches, aspect-preserving height, and nearest-thread matching ([DEV, PhotoToPattern](https://dev.to/wei_zhu_aa04d872270a4135c/how-i-turned-photos-into-printable-cross-stitch-patterns-in-the-browser-2ma7)). Stitch Forge does the match in CIEDE2000 and leaves transparent pixels as unstitched cells ([stitch-forge](https://github.com/Skytuhua/stitch-forge)). Threaded caps the palette around 64, and 30 is the practical ceiling they cite ([DEV, Threaded](https://dev.to/mcinstinct/turning-a-photo-into-a-cross-stitch-pattern-in-the-browser-the-actual-pipeline-1mc4)). A July 2023 canvas-adjacent grid draws a literal X per cell ([Coding Artist](https://codingartistweb.com/2023/07/cross-stitch-app/)). Radius is how many cells are stitched. Strength is cell size and palette size. Both cards work at 3–6 px cells. Larger cells erase a small logo and close letter counters. Not Halftone, not Pixelate, not ASCII: the mark is two crossing stitches, not a dot, a block, or a character.

9. **Opaque-run pixel sort.** In a short band at the pointer, runs of pixels whose brightness sits between two thresholds are reordered by brightness, so ink streaks along the row or column, then the clean buffer is restored on leave. Emergent Mind’s canvas demo sorts column intervals between a low and high brightness (their starting window is 70 to 255), animates the sort, and lets a drag set direction ([Pixel Sorting](https://www.emergentmind.com/visualizations/pixel-sorting)). The same idea as a mouse band: STEMGeeks sorts only a 40 px-tall strip near the pointer, with thresholds 30 and 220 ([creative coding notes](https://stemgeeks.net/@femdev/learn-creative-coding-10-pixel-manipulation-reading-and-writing-image-data)). A full-image app sorts by hue, saturation, lightness, or a channel, horizontally or vertically ([pixel-sort.evelyn.dev / evelynhathaway](https://github.com/evelynhathaway/pixel-sort)). An ICM session maps `mouseX` to the brightness threshold ([session notes](https://github.com/itpresidents/icm-help-sessions-2020/blob/master/session-08/session-08-example.md)). Radius is the band thickness. Strength is how wide the brightness window is. Logos produce short streaks along the mark. Letters smear into the gaps if transparent pixels are sorted with the ink; sort only opaque runs. Not Glitch, not Melt, not VHS: pixels are reordered by luminance, not shifted, stretched, or torn.

10. **Pointer slit-scan.** While the pointer moves, a thin strip of the artwork at the pointer is written into a persisting buffer, photo-finish style, and the buffer fades after leave. The canvas mechanics, written for video, are: keep as many frames as there are strips, copy one strip from each stored frame, stitch them ([thewhodidthis, slit-scan](https://thewhodidthis.com/slit-scan-thing/); implementation [wobble](https://github.com/thewhodidthis/wobble)). A live page uses the cursor as the scanner ([Slit-Scan Interactive](https://silviavaelk.github.io/slit-scanner/)). Another canvas tool moves the slit across video and writes a strip whose width can differ from the slit ([gmpreussner](https://www.gmpreussner.com/vault/experiments/slit-scan-images-in-javascript)). Radius is the slit width. Strength is how long a sample stays and how fast the buffer scrolls. A still logo does nothing if you slit-scan identical frames; the pointer has to move or the buffer stays empty. Sparse letters write mostly transparent strips. Not VHS: VHS tears the current frame and keeps no time buffer.

11. **Voronoi mosaic.** Seeds near the pointer own the pixels closest to them. Each cell is filled with the average color of the artwork inside it, with a dark lead line on the border. Cells ease back to the original pixels on leave. Lumitree’s canvas filter assigns every pixel to the nearest seed by squared distance, averages RGBA per cell, and notes that a Lloyd step (move each seed to its cell’s centroid) stops the mosaic looking like a random shatter ([Lumitree, Voronoi mosaic](https://lumitree.art/blog/voronoi-diagram)). Emergent Mind’s canvas version also measures the distance to the border between the nearest and second-nearest seed, `(dB² - dA²) / (2 * |A - B|)`, and darkens that band; a pane follows the pointer ([Voronoi Mosaic](https://www.emergentmind.com/visualizations/voronoi)). Radius limits which seeds are active. Strength is seed spacing, or the gap opened between cells. Logos with big shapes survive a few dozen seeds. Fine type disappears once a cell is wider than a stroke. Average only opaque pixels or the page background bleeds into the glyph. Not Pixelate: cells are not axis-aligned blocks. Not Shatter: nothing spins away. Not Halftone.

12. **Corner page curl.** The nearest corner lifts and curls back as the pointer approaches that corner, a shadow sits in the crease, and on leave the sheet lies flat. A Canvas 2D implementation exists: curl.js 1.4 adds a page curl and a backside mask to an image, minimum about 64×64, and can change curl size on hover ([curl.js](http://www.netzgesta.de/curl/)). The versions people copy now are WebGL. Canvas UI’s Peel is cursor-driven, with curl radius in CSS pixels, shade, and shine ([Peel](https://canvasui.dev/docs/components/peel)). The html-in-canvas book turn deforms a cylinder and mirrors the back face ([Page Curl / Book Turn](https://html-in-canvas.dev/demos/page-curl-book-turn/)). turngl does the same on a subdivided plane: `x = dist * cos(angle)`, `z = dist * sin(angle)` ([turngl](https://github.com/oguzhanT/turngl)). A 2D card can fake this with `drawImage` strips parallel to the curl, mirroring strips past the crease and painting a gradient shadow. That strip fold is an approximation inferred from those WebGL descriptions plus MDN’s rectangle-only `drawImage`, not a line reading of curl.js. Quality lost: no true cylinder, no lip highlight, backside is a flat flip. Square logos read immediately. A wide headline only curls one corner, which is still a fidget. If the corner is empty margin, the flap is blank; curl the card bounds. Not Bulge, Wave, or 3D tilt.

13. **Verlet cloth sheet.** The artwork is hung on a grid of points tied by distance constraints. The pointer grabs points inside a radius and the sheet sags, then the constraints pull it home on leave. Cylian’s canvas page is a Verlet cloth: integrating from the previous position, then relaxing constraints, with drag inside about 30 px by pinning a point’s current and previous position, and an optional tear ([Cylian cloth](https://cylian.org/canvas/cloth/)). 4rknova’s demo is HTML canvas plus a GUI, titled as Verlet, and the fetched page documents grab, cut, and wind, not the solver ([4rknova cloth](https://www.4rknova.com/demos/cloth_2d/)). A CodePen titled as Verlet cloth applies gravity `{x: 0, y: 0.98}`, friction `0.99`, and on drag sets any point within 30 px to the cursor ([cedarcantab pen](https://codepen.io/cedarcantab/embed/emYNEpZ)). None of these texture a logo. `drawImage` cannot fill a free quad, only a rectangle ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/CanvasRenderingContext2D/drawImage)), so the 2D version slices the bitmap into many thin strips between grid rows. Quality lost versus a WebGL cloth: no perspective-correct quads, weak lighting. A headline hangs convincingly if the top row is pinned. A small logo needs a fine grid or it looks like Jelly. Empty cells still pull, so the whole word sags as one sheet. Not Wave: the displacement is constraints, not a sine. Not Pluck: the links are 2D, not independent strings.

14. **Contour embroidery.** Only the outline is sewn. Short stitches walk the silhouette inside the radius, and the fill stays the original bitmap. Edge pixels come from a Sobel pass ([scanlines](https://github.com/collidingScopes/scanlines), [BestHub](https://www.besthub.dev/articles/how-to-build-a-versatile-canvas-library-with-edge-detection-video-capture-and-particle-effects-96bc31ce2cf1)). Stitch color can come from the same thread-matching step as cross-stitch ([PhotoToPattern](https://dev.to/wei_zhu_aa04d872270a4135c/how-i-turned-photos-into-printable-cross-stitch-patterns-in-the-browser-2ma7)). Walking those edge pixels as dashed tangent segments is an adaptation; no single hover demo retrieved here does that walk. Radius is how far along the contour the needle has traveled. Strength is stitch length and gap. Letters, being outlines, work. A filled logo only grows a seam around the silhouette, so the interior never fidgets. Not sketch: the interior is not converted. Not knit: there is no X grid.

15. **Moving-threshold posterize.** Inside the radius, luminance is crushed to a few flat bands, and the band edges slide as strength or pointer position changes. STEMGeeks includes a threshold filter in the same ImageData loop as pixel sort, but the retrieved excerpt does not show that function’s cutoff ([creative coding notes](https://stemgeeks.net/@femdev/learn-creative-coding-10-pixel-manipulation-reading-and-writing-image-data)). The sourced multi-level formula is Szabados’s, with the Bayer offset removed: quantize each channel into `c` steps ([Bayer Dithering](https://spencerszabados.github.io/blog/2022/bayer-dithering/)). Radius feathers the crush. Strength is the level count and the threshold slide. Flat brand logos barely change, because they are already a few colors; the slide has to move the cut across anti-aliased edges or nothing happens. Those edges become jagged. Not Duotone: there is no two-ink reprint. Not dither: there is no threshold matrix, so bands are flat.

16. **Metaball goop.** Opaque pixels near the pointer become blobs. Each blob contributes `radius² / distance²` to a scalar field; where the sum passes 1 the blobs merge into one gooey shape colored by the home pixel. On leave they separate and snap home. The field and the threshold are the whole algorithm, and a step of a few pixels keeps it cheap ([slicker.me metaballs](https://slicker.me/javascript/metaballs/metaballs-tutorial.htm); same field into `ImageData` at [webdevpuneet](https://webdevpuneet.com/ui-snippets/canvas-metaball-blobs/)). Smoother borders come from marching squares on that field ([huynonstop/metaball-canvas](https://github.com/huynonstop/metaball-canvas)). Radius is which pixels join the field. Strength is blob radius and the attract speed toward the pointer. Chunky logos work. Fine type fails: if the blob radius exceeds the letter spacing, “ill” becomes one puddle. Not Pixel burst: circles merge. Not Black hole: there is no orbit. Not Sand: nothing falls to a floor.

### Inferences

- Shared implementation shape for the top eight: draw the bitmap into an offscreen buffer once, and on each frame touch only the rectangle around the pointer. Arcaweb already does this for the droplet, and it is what keeps a card at interactive rates ([Arcaweb](https://www.arcaweb.ch/en/recreate-liquid-glass-effect-html-canvas)).
- Anything that averages or sorts must ignore alpha below a small cutoff. A particle hover gist skips pixels under an alpha threshold before spawning ([interactive particle image hover](https://gist.github.com/Xaypanya/2477a657709ed5c783d52a894e5f74b0)). That gist’s motion is a repel-and-spring, which is Scatter, so the motion is not a candidate; the alpha test is.
- The lab’s “return to rest” rules out in-place smudge, sort, and slit-scan unless a clean buffer is kept and crossfaded back. The paint demo and the pixel-sort apps are destructive ([Simple Paint Demo](https://math.hws.edu/graphicsbook/demos/c2/SimplePaintDemo.html), [pixel-sort](https://github.com/evelynhathaway/pixel-sort)).
- Ranks 1–8 are the ones to build first if both cards must feel good. Ranks 9–13 are worth one card type. Ranks 14–16 are easy to mistake for a success on a chunky logo and a failure on the headline.

These showed up in searches and should be rejected as cousins or as out of bounds:

- **Grid source-offset “liquid distortion”** (cells call the 9-argument `drawImage` and shift source coordinates away from the pointer, then ease the offset back) is a January 2026 tutorial, and it is Bulge plus Jelly ([freefrontend, 2026-01-18](https://freefrontend.com/code/interactive-canvas-liquid-image-distortion-2026-01-18/)).
- **Full-frame chromatic shift** of one channel by N pixels is the gist above, and on a card it reads as Glitch without the slices.
- **Pixi displacement maps** and Three.js fluid hovers need a dependency ([CodePen tispeco](https://codepen.io/tispeco/pen/pEOVLg), [merttoka gist](https://gist.github.com/merttoka/848821ab957bedf8354334b2af01f88d)).
- **Repelling image particles** that spring home are Scatter ([particle hover gist](https://gist.github.com/Xaypanya/2477a657709ed5c783d52a894e5f74b0)).
- **Canvas UI Shatter, VHS, Glitch, Asciify, Ripple, Magnify** match cards already in the lab. Shatter there is also WebGL ([CSS Script roundup](https://www.cssscript.com/canvas-ui/), [Shatter](https://canvasui.dev/docs/components/shatter)).
- **Codrops sliced hover, 21 June 2023**, reveals an image as clipped slices with GSAP. It is a transition, not a pointer fidget, and it is DOM rather than canvas ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2023/06/21/coding-the-sliced-image-hover-effect-from-quai-network/)).
- **Pointillism and CMYK plate offsets** were not given their own entries: the first is Halftone, the second collapses into either Glitch or the chromatic lens.
- **Another gravity well** that is not Black hole, Sand, or Drop did not turn up as its own demo. Metaball goop is the sourced “gather at the pointer” that does not orbit and does not fall to a floor. A packed rubble pile with contact constraints was not found; see Gaps.

## What is the actual 2D algorithm, and what fails on small logos or sparse letters?

### Takeaway

Every effect here is either a sample remap (`getImageData` or `drawImage` strips), a threshold or sort on those samples, or a small particle or constraint system drawn back on top of the bitmap. They fail when the effect’s smallest feature, the Bayer cell, stitch, Voronoi seed, metaball radius, cloth quad, or channel offset, is larger than the logo or the letter stroke, and when transparent gaps are treated as ink.

### Cited Findings

Algorithms in one pass, same order as the catalog. “Fails when” is the part that matters for these two card types.

- **Chromatic rim lens.** For each pixel inside the disc, compute the direction away from the center and a distance fraction. Read red, green, and blue from three source positions along that direction. The gist’s real code does a simpler thing on the whole frame: `data[i] = data[i + 4 * intensity]` with `i` starting at `phase % 4`, so one channel slides right by `intensity` pixels ([gist](https://gist.github.com/lqt0223/8a258b68ae1c032fa1fb1e26c4965e8d)). Use the three-sample version, and scale the offset by the distance fraction so the middle of the lens is clean. Fails when the rim offset is more than a few pixels on a logo under about 64 px, or larger than the stroke on a headline. Also fails if the shift wraps onto the next row at the edge of the buffer, which the gist’s flat index math will do.

- **Rim glass.** One predrawn buffer. Per droplet pixel, `norm` is the min distance to the rounded rectangle’s edge divided by an inner radius. `f = 1 + k * (1 - norm)^2` in the published code block. Sample `center + (pixel - center) * f`. Negative `k` magnifies, and the magnify is zero where `norm` is 1 ([Arcaweb](https://www.arcaweb.ch/en/recreate-liquid-glass-effect-html-canvas)). Add a white rim from the same `norm`, not from a second warp. Fails when the droplet covers the whole card (it becomes Lens), when `k` is so large that samples leave the bitmap, and on small counters in letters, which pinch shut. Their own note says to compute the droplet in a larger buffer and scale it down, or the rim looks pixelated.

- **Bayer lens.** Grayscale or per channel. Threshold = matrix cell divided by matrix area, times 255. Binary: on or off if luminance beats it ([javaspring.net](https://www.javaspring.net/blog/monochrome-dithering-in-javascript-bayer-atkinson-floyd-steinberg/)). Multi-level: add the matrix value before the floor-divide, as in Szabados with `c` levels ([2022 writeup](https://spencerszabados.github.io/blog/2022/bayer-dithering/)). Mix with the original by a smooth falloff of distance over radius. Fails when the matrix tile is larger than the mark, and when thin grey anti-aliasing is the only thing describing a stroke: 1-bit dither deletes it. Floyd–Steinberg and Atkinson are the error-diffusion alternatives in the same javaspring article; they smear error into neighboring pixels, which on sparse letters leaks dots into the counters. Prefer Bayer for a card.

- **Smudge.** Keep `clean` and `live` buffers. On move, copy a patch from `live` into a brush canvas, feather it, stamp it along the segment at alpha equal to strength, then continue. On leave, fade `live` back to `clean` ([paint demo](https://math.hws.edu/graphicsbook/demos/c2/SimplePaintDemo.html), [Stack Overflow](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/28197378/html5-canvas-javascript-smudge-brush-tool)). Fails when the brush radius exceeds the stroke, because the stamp averages the counter into the letter. A fast swipe with a large step size leaves visible discs instead of a smear.

- **Slat blinds.** Cut the bitmap into horizontal bands with `drawImage(image, sx, sy, sw, sh, dx, dy, dw, dh)` ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/CanvasRenderingContext2D/drawImage)). For each band, `scaleY = 1 - strength * falloff(distance from pointer to the band)`. Draw around the band’s vertical center. The CSS sources rotate the slat in 3D instead ([the new code](https://thenewcode.com/776/3d-venetian-blind-image-transition-in-pure-css)). Fails when `scaleY` hits 0 through the middle of a word: the line of type disappears. Clamp the minimum scale to about 0.15. A logo with a horizontal wordmark loses the word the same way; a symbol-only logo mostly survives.

- **Pencil sketch.** Once: grey, invert, box- or stack-blur the invert, color-dodge against grey ([JsonKit](https://jsokit.com/blog/posts/image-to-sketch-gaussian-blur-and-color-dodge-blending-with-canvas/)). Each frame, destination-in or lerp using a radial mask. Fails on flat color logos with no internal edge: dodge goes white. Fails if the blur radius is tiny: the formula’s author notes that without blur the result is a white frame. Letters work because the stroke is the edge.

- **Ink smoke.** Emitter at opaque pixels inside the radius. Particle life decays, alpha follows life, a small upward force, a little horizontal noise, draw a radial sprite multiplied by the source RGB ([Middlebury fall 2024](https://www.cs.middlebury.edu/~candrews/classes/cs467-f24/tutorials/tutorial06-smoke), [smoke.js](https://www.cssscript.com/interactive-smoke-effect-javascript-canvas-smoke-js/)). The tutorial’s mouse term is `G / max(distance², 50)` and can attract or repel; for this card use a weak repel so puffs peel off the cursor, or skip it so they only rise. Fails if spawning deletes the source pixel (that is Sand). Fails on hairline type because too few pixels pass the alpha test. Cap the particle list; the tutorial warns an uncapped emitter will stall the page.

- **Cross-stitch.** `drawImage` the artwork into a grid of cell width by cell height, quantize opaque cells, draw an X in the cell’s thread color, reveal cells whose center is inside the radius ([PhotoToPattern](https://dev.to/wei_zhu_aa04d872270a4135c/how-i-turned-photos-into-printable-cross-stitch-patterns-in-the-browser-2ma7), [stitch-forge](https://github.com/Skytuhua/stitch-forge)). Fails when cell size is coarser than the stroke: a letter becomes one or two stitches and the counter disappears. Transparent cells must stay unstitched or the gaps between letters fill in.

- **Pixel sort.** For each row in the band, walk x. Start a run when alpha is high and luminance is inside `[low, high]`. On run end, sort that run by luminance and write it back. Do not sort across alpha holes ([Emergent Mind](https://www.emergentmind.com/visualizations/pixel-sorting), band example [STEMGeeks](https://stemgeeks.net/@femdev/learn-creative-coding-10-pixel-manipulation-reading-and-writing-image-data)). Fails on a tiny logo because runs are a few pixels and the streak is invisible. Fails on letters if the run includes the transparent gap: the stem’s pixels get distributed across the counter. Vertical sort of a whole column looks like Melt; keep the band short so it does not.

- **Slit-scan.** A ring of strips. On pointer move, copy a slit-width column or row from the clean bitmap at the pointer into the next slot of an output buffer. Fade the buffer to the clean image when the pointer leaves ([thewhodidthis](https://thewhodidthis.com/slit-scan-thing/), cursor scan [silviavaelk](https://silviavaelk.github.io/slit-scanner/)). Fails on a still pointer, on a logo smaller than a few slits, and on letters whose ink does not cross the slit. Classic video slit-scan of a static image reproduces the image; the time axis has to be the pointer’s motion.

- **Voronoi mosaic.** Seeds jitter inside the radius. For each pixel in a half-resolution buffer, nearest seed wins, accumulate color, divide by count, optionally darken if the border distance is under about 1.5 px ([Lumitree](https://lumitree.art/blog/voronoi-diagram), border formula [Emergent Mind](https://www.emergentmind.com/visualizations/voronoi)). Fails when seed count is low on a small logo (the mark becomes three blobs) and when cells are wider than a stroke. Skip transparent pixels in the average.

- **Page curl.** Strength maps to how far the nearest corner has peeled, from 0 at rest to a max of about a third of the card. Strips on the peeled side are drawn mirrored and shortened; a dark gradient sits on the crease. Hover-driven curl size is what curl.js exposes ([curl.js](http://www.netzgesta.de/curl/)). The cylinder formula `x = dist cos θ`, `z = dist sin θ` is the WebGL version ([turngl](https://github.com/oguzhanT/turngl)). Fails when the peeled region is mostly padding, and when the curl crosses the middle of a short word and hides every letter. A 64×64 floor is curl.js’s own minimum.

- **Verlet cloth.** Points in a grid, rest length equal to spacing. Each step: velocity is position minus previous position, add a small gravity or a grab impulse, then for several iterations push linked points back to rest length. Grab: if distance to pointer is under the radius, set position and previous position to the pointer so velocity does not explode ([Cylian](https://cylian.org/canvas/cloth/), [CodePen](https://codepen.io/cedarcantab/embed/emYNEpZ)). Draw by sampling a source row into each deformed row. Fails when the grid is coarser than the logo’s features, and when gravity is left on after leave so the sheet never returns; on leave, blend toward the rest grid or set gravity to 0 and increase stiffness. Sparse letters sag as a rectangle, including the holes, unless links that sit entirely on transparent pixels are omitted. Omitting them lets glyphs tear apart, which may be desirable and is closer to Shatter if they also spin. Keep the links if the goal is one sheet.

- **Contour embroidery.** Sobel on the alpha or luminance, threshold, then from each edge pixel step along the tangent `(gy, -gx)` and draw a short line in the pixel color ([Sobel sources above](https://github.com/collidingScopes/scanlines)). Fails on soft, blurry logo edges where the gradient never passes the threshold, and on filled marks where the user expected the inside to react.

- **Posterize.** `steps = strength mapped to 2..6`. `out = round(value / 255 * (steps - 1)) / (steps - 1) * 255`, applied inside the mask. The Bayer article is the sourced quantization; drop the matrix term `T` for flat bands ([Szabados](https://spencerszabados.github.io/blog/2022/bayer-dithering/)). Fails on logos that are already flat color. The only motion is the jagged edge where anti-aliasing crosses a cut. Sparse letters show that jagged edge clearly, which is the better of the two cards.

- **Metaball goop.** Particles sampled on a stride from opaque pixels. While the pointer is down in the card, particles inside the radius accelerate toward it. Field at a coarse grid: sum `r² / (dx² + dy²)`. Paint the cell if the sum exceeds 1, using the nearest particle’s color ([slicker.me](https://slicker.me/javascript/metaballs/metaballs-tutorial.htm)). Fails when `r` is larger than the gap between letters, and when the coarse cell is larger than the logo detail. Also fails if every opaque pixel is a metaball: a headline becomes thousands of field terms. Use a stride, and only promote pixels inside the radius.

### Inferences

- Two different failure families. Feature-size failures: dither tile, stitch cell, Voronoi seed, metaball radius, cloth spacing, channel offset. Alpha failures: sort, average, cloth links, and goop treating the gap between letters as material. Fix the second family with an alpha test before any mix. Fix the first by tying the feature size to strength and capping it against the smaller of logo width and stroke width.
- Small logos want ranks 1, 2, 4, 5, and 7, because those do not impose a tile. Headlines want ranks 3, 6, 8, and 14, because letters are edges and stitches. Ranks 12 and 13 should be allowed to look different on the two cards rather than forced to match.
- `getImageData` on the full card every frame is the cost that will drop a card off 60 fps. Glass, dither, posterize, sketch mask, and smudge can run on the radius rectangle only. Voronoi and metaballs need a half or third resolution buffer. Pixel sort should stay on the band, which is how the STEMGeeks example is already written.

### Gaps

- Stroke width of the lab’s headline font was not measured, so “under one em” and “3–6 px cells” are relative guides, not measured against the live cards.
- No benchmark of `getImageData` cost on these card sizes was run.
- The square-versus-cube disagreement in the Arcaweb article was not resolved by a second source.

## Which 2023–2026 canvas techniques are newly popular?

### Takeaway

From 2023 through October 2026 the pointer effects that gathered new writeups, and that this lab does not already ship, are liquid-glass refraction with a chromatic rim, a cursor dither lens, page peel, textile pattern generators, and a fresh smoke-tutorial pass. Several of the most visible 2025–2026 demos are WebGL or depend on Chrome’s experimental html-in-canvas API. The January 2026 canvas “liquid distortion” tutorial is real and new, and it duplicates Bulge and Jelly.

### Cited Findings

- **Liquid glass, as a refraction look, is the clearest new cluster.** A Canvas 2D droplet remap with rim-weighted sampling, a Fresnel rim, and a reflection is written up at Arcaweb; the fetched page has no publication date ([Arcaweb](https://www.arcaweb.ch/en/recreate-liquid-glass-effect-html-canvas)). Carson Ye describes the 2025–2026 web pattern as cloning or sampling what is behind a panel, bending UVs from a soft edge normal, then adding tint, highlight, and a chromatic split, and he is explicit that a DOM clone cannot see canvas pixels so the glass has to sample a canvas ([Carson Ye](https://carsonye.com/articles/where-webgl-meet-liquid-glass/)). liquid-glass-canvas is WebGL: rounded-rectangle SDF, edge-normal displacement, a `chroma` amount, tint, and glint, at a claimed 60 fps, and it refuses the `getImageData` route as too slow ([repo](https://github.com/Whynotmetoo/liquid-glass-canvas)). Canvas UI’s Glass lens is also a shader, with per-wavelength indices of refraction, and it wants `drawElementImage` where the browser has html-in-canvas ([Glass](https://canvasui.dev/docs/components/glass)). What 2D loses: spectral dispersion becomes three samples instead of a wavelength integral, the rim highlight is a painted gradient, and there is no real frosted blur unless a separate blur is added.

- **A dither lens on the cursor, not a full-frame filter, is the new framing of an old algorithm.** Bayer, Atkinson, and Floyd–Steinberg on canvas `ImageData` are taught as webcam filters ([javaspring.net](https://www.javaspring.net/blog/monochrome-dithering-in-javascript-bayer-atkinson-floyd-steinberg/)); Szabados’s writeup is 2022, just before the window ([Szabados](https://spencerszabados.github.io/blog/2022/bayer-dithering/)). Canvas UI’s current Retro Dither is the lens form: radius, softness, pixel size, level count, pattern `bayer | halftone | hatch | dash`, plus a trail, and it is a shader ([Retro Dither](https://canvasui.dev/docs/components/retro-dither)). The `halftone` pattern in that list is the lab’s existing Halftone and should not be rebuilt. Bayer and hatch are the new parts. Hatch overlaps the pencil-sketch entry.

- **Page peel and cloth are in the current canvas-effect catalogs, mostly as WebGL.** Canvas UI documents Peel (cursor, curl radius) and the CSS Script roundup lists Cloth, Bubble, Glass, Frost, Bend, Droplets, and Displacement alongside older effects the lab already has ([CSS Script](https://www.cssscript.com/canvas-ui/), [Peel](https://canvasui.dev/docs/components/peel)). html-in-canvas’s book turn, fetched as a current demo, rasterizes HTML with `drawElementImage` and then curls in a fragment shader ([demo](https://html-in-canvas.dev/demos/page-curl-book-turn/)). The 2D fallback remains curl.js and a strip fold ([curl.js](http://www.netzgesta.de/curl/)). Frost, as an ice-melt-and-refreeze under the cursor, showed up only as a WebGL component in that roundup. No credible Canvas 2D frost that is clearly different from Dissolve was found.

- **Textile conversion in the browser is a 2023–2026 theme, not a hover theme.** Coding Artist’s cross-stitch grid is July 2023 ([article](https://codingartistweb.com/2023/07/cross-stitch-app/)). PhotoToPattern, Stitch Forge, and Threaded are client-side photo-to-pattern tools using canvas downsample and thread matching; the DEV pages and the Stitch Forge README were fetched without a reliable publication date on the page ([PhotoToPattern](https://dev.to/wei_zhu_aa04d872270a4135c/how-i-turned-photos-into-printable-cross-stitch-patterns-in-the-browser-2ma7), [stitch-forge](https://github.com/Skytuhua/stitch-forge), [Threaded](https://dev.to/mcinstinct/turning-a-photo-into-a-cross-stitch-pattern-in-the-browser-the-actual-pipeline-1mc4)). None of them are pointer hovers. The hover, stitches sewing in under the cursor, is the lab-shaped use of that pipeline.

- **Smoke got a classroom canvas treatment in fall 2024**, with upward force, mouse `G / r²`, and stacked soft sprites ([Middlebury](https://www.cs.middlebury.edu/~candrews/classes/cs467-f24/tutorials/tutorial06-smoke)). smoke.js is older, last changelog note April 2019, and is the no-library version ([CSS Script](https://www.cssscript.com/interactive-smoke-effect-javascript-canvas-smoke-js/)). Popularity here means it is still being taught, not that it is new.

- **Slice hovers had a Codrops moment on 21 June 2023**, GSAP clip-paths of one image, as a grid-item hover ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2023/06/21/coding-the-sliced-image-hover-effect-from-quai-network/)). That is the blinds family in spirit and a DOM transition in practice. It is not a reason to add another GSAP card; the canvas slat squash covers the missing visual.

- **A dated 18 January 2026 canvas tutorial** does interactive image distortion by shifting `drawImage` source rectangles away from the pointer and easing them back ([freefrontend](https://freefrontend.com/code/interactive-canvas-liquid-image-distortion-2026-01-18/)). It is new, it is Canvas 2D, and it should not be built. It matches Bulge and Jelly.

- **Pixel sorting and slit-scan did not look new.** The retrieved tutorials and apps present them as established glitch and photo-finish techniques ([Emergent Mind](https://www.emergentmind.com/visualizations/pixel-sorting), [thewhodidthis](https://thewhodidthis.com/slit-scan-thing/), [gmpreussner](https://www.gmpreussner.com/vault/experiments/slit-scan-images-in-javascript)). They are missing from the lab, which is not the same as being a 2023–2026 fashion.

### Inferences

- If the goal is “what people are making right now” that this lab lacks, build the rim glass and the chromatic lens as one card or as a pair, then the Bayer lens. Those are the 2025–2026 looks that still have a honest Canvas 2D path.
- If the goal is “fidget on a logo and on letters,” glass and chroma still rank at the top, and dither, smudge, and blinds are the next builds even though blinds and smudge are older than the window.
- WebGL catalogs are a bad menu for this repo. Canvas UI, liquid-glass-canvas, turngl, and the html-in-canvas book turn all assume a shader or an experimental DOM-in-canvas API. Use them as a picture of the effect, then implement the 2D column in the catalog.

### Gaps

- No 2024–2026 “best canvas cursor effects” roundup other than the Canvas UI / CSS Script catalog was found. That catalog mixes WebGL, html-in-canvas, and effects the lab already ships, so it is not a clean census of Canvas 2D.
- Publication dates for the Arcaweb article, the DEV stitch posts, Carson Ye’s essay, and the Canvas UI docs were not on the fetched pages.
- CodePen search results came back mostly as shells without the pen’s source, except where a writeup or an embed quoted the script. Pen popularity counts were not available.
- No source dated 2023–2026 was found for a gravity-well variant that is visually distinct from Black hole, Sand, Drop, and metaball goop.
